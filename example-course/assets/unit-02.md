## concept: sour-vs-bitter
- quiz: Sour points to which extraction problem, and bitter to which? | a: sour = under-extracted (too little pulled out); bitter = over-extracted (too much, including the harsh late compounds) | distractor: M1
- quiz: A shot is sour. Your instinct is to add water to soften it. What does that actually do? | a: nothing to the sourness — it dilutes strength while extraction stays low; the fix is more extraction, not less strength | distractor: M1
- example: worked | Taste in the order the compounds dissolve: acids come out first, sugars next, bitter phenolics last. Stop too early and you have acids without the sugars to balance them — sour. Run too long and the late phenolics dominate — bitter. The taste tells you where on that timeline you stopped.

## concept: brew-ratio
- quiz: Define brew ratio and name what it primarily controls. | a: liquid mass out divided by dry coffee mass in; it primarily sets strength | distractor: M1
- quiz: 18g in, 36g out is what ratio, and what would moving to 1:1.5 do to the cup? | a: 1:2; moving to 1:1.5 concentrates it — higher strength, and slightly lower yield from the shorter run | distractor: M1
- example: worked | 18g dose, 36g out = 1:2, the common starting point. Pull 27g out (1:1.5) and you get a more concentrated cup with less total extraction; pull 54g (1:3) and you get a weaker cup with more extraction — which is exactly how strength and yield come apart.

## concept: channeling
- quiz: A shot runs fast AND tastes both sour and bitter at once. What does that combination indicate? | a: channeling — water raced through one path (over-extracting it) while under-extracting the rest, so you taste both faults together | distractor: M1
- quiz: You grind finer to fix a fast shot and it gets worse and more uneven. What is the likely real problem? | a: distribution, not grind — an uneven or cracked puck is channeling; fix the prep first | distractor: M1
- example: worked | Water is lazy: it takes the cheapest route. A crack, or a patch that is dense next to one that is loose, gives it one. That route over-extracts to bitterness while the rest of the bed is barely touched and stays sour. The signature is the contradiction — both faults in one cup.

## rubric: sour-vs-bitter
- claim: sour indicates under-extraction and bitter indicates over-extraction
- claim: the cause is the order compounds dissolve — acids early, sugars mid, harsh phenolics late
- claim: the corrective lever is extraction (grind and contact time), not dilution
- avoid: M1
- avoid: M2
